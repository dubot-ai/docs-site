## Authority should be designed, not assumed

An action layer is useful because it can affect the product. That is also why its authority must be explicit, narrow, and observable.

Dubot's intended governance model begins with named capabilities and agreed control points. It does not begin with unrestricted product access.

## How actions are governed

Every action carries a policy that decides how it may run.

| Policy | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Auto | May run without a confirmation step |
| Confirm | Requires customer or agent approval before running |
| Never | Is not available to the agent |

Policy is attached to the intended effect. Opening a section, preparing a form, and submitting a consequential change can therefore use different controls. A customer evaluation must document any action categories that are forced to confirm in that environment.

## Two execution boundaries

### Browser execution

Browserbot operates inside the customer's signed-in product session. It inherits the permissions enforced by the product and uses relevant page, journey, and user context to resolve the next step.

Depending on journey policy, Browserbot can explain, navigate, prepare information, or interact with a product control. A consequential effect can pause for explicit approval. After execution, the journey observes the expected product state and reports completion, failure, recovery, or safe stopping.

### Connected action execution

A connected action uses a reviewed action contract. The customer's product remains responsible for authorization, input validation, business rules, and the resulting source-of-truth state. Dubot applies the configured action policy and records the result available to the journey.

These routes can appear in one journey, but their data flow and enforcement points are different. They should be reviewed separately.

## What the action layer can see

Through a connected product, Dubot can process relevant page context, elements available to the journey, interactions, and the product responses needed to continue or verify an approved task. This does not grant hidden or privileged access beyond the connected surface and signed-in user's permissions.

Relevant journey context may be transmitted to Dubot and the configured model provider. Each customer should review the context included, data minimization, model-provider configuration, and any downstream processing before exposure.

## How support data is handled

When a support conversation is used to measure or improve deflection, that data is read-only and kept to a minimum footprint. Conversation content should be de-identified or reduced to outcome-level signal, according to what the customer's security team prefers. Connector scopes should be requested at the minimum needed for a specific use case, and narrowed when a smaller scope is enough.

<Note>
  Actions are reviewable before use, in the Actions interface or over
  [MCP](/integrations/mcp), so a security or engineering team can inspect
  exactly what an action does before it is attached to an agent.
</Note>

## Technical review checklist

Before customer use moves beyond a controlled test, Dubot and the customer should document:

- environments and domains in scope;
- user identity and session assumptions;
- credentials or tokens, if any, and how they are stored;
- the minimum data passed into and out of a journey;
- actions that are allowed, confirmation-gated, or prohibited;
- logging, retention, and access expectations;
- failure, recovery, and support ownership;
- testing and release approval.

The review should also distinguish configuration retention, chat retention, action-log retention, customer-visible audit history, and backup deletion behavior. These categories may have different terms.

## Rollback and reversal

Dubot does not promise a universal rollback mechanism. Reversing a completed change requires a product-specific compensating action or a supported UI flow. A journey that cannot establish a safe reversal path should stop, report the outcome, and route the case for review.

## A practical evaluation principle

The first question is not "Can the system click anything?" It is "Can this one approved customer outcome be completed safely, predictably, and visibly?"
